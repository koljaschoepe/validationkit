# 1 — Positioning

**Thesis:** Find the wedge no funded competitor has bothered with. Position against the *category leader's blind spot*, not against their feature list.

## Concession-then-Critique

**Concession:** Most founders pick a wedge that's *adjacent* to something they're already good at. That's healthy — domain expertise compounds, and a wedge you can't credibly serve is a wedge that doesn't matter.

**Critique:** Adjacent is the floor, not the ceiling. The mistake is stopping there. A wedge that's adjacent to your skills *and* covered by a funded incumbent is a wedge you lose to feature parity. You need the second axis: adjacent to your skills AND under-served by everyone with cash to throw at it.

## The 2×2 you actually need

```
              Easy for you             Hard for you
            +------------------+------------------+
Crowded     |  Don't bother    |  Don't bother    |
incumbent   |  (you'll lose    |  (worse + you're |
            |   on resources)  |   slow)          |
            +------------------+------------------+
Funded      |  Don't bother    |  Don't bother    |
adjacency   |  (someone with   |  (extra hard +   |
            |   money clones)  |   funded near)   |
            +------------------+------------------+
Under-      |  ← YOUR WEDGE    |  Don't bother    |
served      |  (here)          |  (no skill leverage) |
            +------------------+------------------+
```

The bottom-left cell is where solo wedges work. Everywhere else, the math is against you.

## 3 questions before you commit

1. **Who has tried to solve this and gone away?** If nobody tried, the demand probably isn't there. If 3 funded teams tried and stuck around, you're a feature-parity fight. If 3 funded teams tried and gave up, that's your wedge — and you need to learn *why* they gave up before you bet on it.
2. **What's the unfunded substitute today?** Excel, Notion, a Slack thread, "doing it manually." If there's no substitute, your problem isn't real yet. If the substitute is universal, you have to be 10× better, not just 2×.
3. **What's the smallest user-story that makes someone trade their substitute for you?** Write it as one sentence. If it takes more than one sentence, you don't have a wedge yet — you have a feature pile.

## Skeptic-Mentor counter-example

A founder pitched ValidationKit a YC-style "AI for HR onboarding" idea. Adjacent to her skills (10 years HR-tech), clearly demanded (Gusto has 200k customers), and her wedge was "but for remote-only teams."

**Critique:** "Remote-only" isn't a wedge — it's a segmentation. Gusto already serves remote-only customers with the same product. The right question wasn't "what category am I in?" but "what does Gusto refuse to ship for the next 18 months that remote-only teams desperately need?" After two interviews she found the actual wedge: async-friendly onboarding evidence trails for compliance audits. That's a wedge a funded incumbent ignores until it's at $20M ARR.

## Done-when

You can write your wedge in this exact format and not flinch:

> **For** [specific ICP],  
> **who** [does this awkward thing today],  
> **we** [the one-sentence user-story],  
> **unlike** [the substitute],  
> **because** [the second axis — what funded incumbents ignore].

If the "unlike" reads like a feature ("we have GraphQL," "we're faster"), you haven't found the wedge yet. Iterate.
