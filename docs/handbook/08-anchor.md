# 8 — Anchor

**Thesis:** Land the cash engine *before* you scale the product. Productized services pay for the runway that lets you build the SaaS.

## Concession-then-Critique

**Concession:** Founders are told that "service businesses don't scale" and "real founders build product." So they refuse to do consulting / sprints / agency work. They want SaaS revenue or nothing.

**Critique:** SaaS-or-nothing is how solo founders run out of runway in M6. A $4,500 productized sprint at 1/month buys you 4 months of one engineer's salary. Two sprints/month replaces a small team's worth of cash. The productized service isn't a distraction from SaaS — it's the *fuel* that lets you build the SaaS without VC.

The trick is: **productize** the service. Not custom consulting. Not endless retainers. A fixed 2-week sprint with 80% standard deliverables and 20% custom. Pre-priced. Pre-scoped.

## The 5 rules of the productized sprint

1. **Fixed scope.** "2-week sprint, 5 deliverables." If the customer wants extra, it's a second sprint, not a scope-creep.
2. **Fixed price.** $4,500. Not "starting at." Not "let's discuss." The number is on the page.
3. **80% standard, 20% custom.** The 80% is reusable across customers. The 20% is what makes the customer feel served.
4. **Async-first delivery.** Slack + Loom + 2 sync calls (Day 1, Day 14). Not 1 hour/day on Zoom.
5. **Sprint output ≠ ongoing retainer.** Sell the next sprint, not a 6-month engagement.

## How many sprints to validate the anchor?

The PRD Phase-0 number: **2 paid Validation-Sprints + 5 Agency-LOIs** (Criterion #3). That's the bar.

Below 2 paid sprints: the anchor doesn't work — either the wedge isn't real or the price isn't right.

5 LOIs but 0 sprints: the price is right but you can't close. Adjust offer / outreach.

2 sprints, 0 LOIs: you can close one-offs but can't predict pipeline. Build the pipeline before you scale.

2 sprints + 5 LOIs: anchor validated. Now the SaaS-tier work is funded by predictable cash. Phase 1 starts.

## The math (real numbers)

| Phase | Sprints/month | Cash/month | Solo time spent | Time-on-SaaS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 0 (M0–M3) | 0–1 | $0–$4.5k | 30h/wk | 25h/wk |
| Phase 1 (M3–M9) | 1–2 | $4.5k–$9k | 25h/wk | 30h/wk |
| Phase 2 (M9–M18) | 1 | $4.5k | 15h/wk | 40h/wk |
| Phase 3 (M18+) | 0 (or productize as a "Founders' Studio" course) | — | 0h | 55h/wk |

Sprint cadence taper. The anchor is heaviest early, when SaaS revenue is small. As MRR compounds, you can taper. By Phase 3, the anchor is *optional*.

## Skeptic-Mentor counter-example

A founder refused consulting work for the entire first year. "I'm not a consultant. I'm a founder." She had $40k in savings and a $19/mo product. Month 6: $1,140 MRR. Month 9: $1,890 MRR. Month 11: out of money. Quit.

Another founder, same product, took 2 productized sprints in Month 1 at $3,500 each. $7,000 cash + 2 real customers + 2 referrals. By Month 6: 1 sprint/month standing + $4,200 MRR. By Month 12: solo runway secured, MRR $14,000. SaaS-first wasn't the right strategy — *anchor-first* was.

The first founder didn't lack discipline. She lacked an anchor.

## Done-when

- Productized-sprint offer page is live with fixed price, fixed scope, fixed timeline.
- 1 sprint has been delivered end-to-end, with a customer testimonial.
- Pipeline of 5 prospects with > 30% close-rate intent.
- You can say with a straight face: "I'm not a consultant — I sell a productized 2-week sprint."

If the productized service still feels like consulting, it isn't productized yet.
