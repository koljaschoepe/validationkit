# 3 — Channels

**Thesis:** Validate channels with the same rigor as the product. Where your ICP *says* they hang out is rarely where they actually decide to buy.

## Concession-then-Critique

**Concession:** Channel intuition usually starts with "where do I hang out as a similar persona?" That's the right starting point. Founder–channel-fit is a real edge.

**Critique:** Hanging out ≠ buying. Indie-Hacker Twitter is a great place to learn. It's a bad place to convert. The discovery and the decision happen in different rooms — and the decision room is usually quieter.

## The 3-question channel audit

For every channel you're considering, answer these three questions with specifics (not vibes):

1. **Where did the last 5 customers you respect *discover* you?**
   - Not "buyers in general." Customers you respect. The ones you'd want 100 of.
   - If you don't have 5 customers yet, ask 5 founders 2 years ahead of you in the same wedge.
2. **Where did the *decision-maker* sign the contract or hit "Subscribe"?**
   - Discovery and decision are different rooms. Twitter → newsletter → cold-mail → close is 4 channels, not 1.
3. **What's the substitute channel that *funded* competitors use?**
   - If they're all on Google Ads, that's a tax wall. Don't enter.
   - If they're all on partnerships and you're a solo, that's a moat against speed. Pick a different channel.

## The channels that empirically work for solo / agency-tier ValidationKit-shaped products

Per Track-D1 v4 research (8 of 8 comp-founders, May 2026), zero solo-founders in the comp-set grew via LinkedIn cold-outreach for sub-$5k-ACV tiers. The empirically-working channels for similar product shapes:

| Channel | When it works | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| **Build-in-Public** (X / Mastodon / LinkedIn) | When the founder is the product's expert and posts daily for 6+ months. | 30 min/day, daily. |
| **SEO content** (long-form blog, comparison pages) | When the buyer Google-searches the problem (not the category). | 2 days/week, compounding. |
| **PLG / free OSS** | When the OSS gives a real win in 5 minutes. | All-in or skip. |
| **Productized service** ($4500-tier) | When the buyer doesn't want to learn the tool, they want the outcome. | Direct outreach, warm intros only. |
| **Conference / podcast appearances** | When you need to establish credibility with a niche. | 1 month per appearance to land it. |
| **Cold email** (Sub-$5k-ACV) | NOT a primary channel. Reply rates: 0.5–2% in this segment. Don't lead with it. |
| **LinkedIn cold-DMs** (Sub-$5k-ACV) | NOT a primary channel. Empirically broken per Track-D1. |

For Phase-0 of *this* product: **Build-in-Public + warm intros + content** = 80% of attention. SEO is a Phase-1 investment. Conferences are M9+. Cold-DM is forbidden by PRD constraint #3 (Legitimate Channels Only).

## How to validate a channel in 4 weeks (not 4 months)

1. **Week 1:** Set up the channel cleanly. Profile, bio, first 5 posts that demonstrate the wedge. *Do not* link a product yet.
2. **Week 2:** Engage with 30 specific accounts in your ICP. Comment, don't pitch. Track who responds.
3. **Week 3:** Publish 1 long-form (blog or thread) that *answers* a question your interviews surfaced. Track who reads it.
4. **Week 4:** Send a soft offer to the 5 most engaged. "I'm running 5 validation sprints at $X. Interested?" Track who buys.

Outcomes by Week 4:
- 0 paid → channel is wrong. Pick another.
- 1 paid + 5 conversations → channel is right but small. Compound.
- 2+ paid → you found your channel. Now build the system around it.

## Skeptic-Mentor counter-example

A founder picked LinkedIn cold-outreach for a Solopreneur tool at $19/mo. Sent 600 DMs. Got 18 replies (3% — within the 3.2% Hunter.io 2026 median we cite in PRD §1). Zero closes.

She moved to Build-in-Public: posted daily about the same wedge for 60 days. Got 2 warm intros, both closed at full price within a week.

The 600 DMs took ~25 hours and burned her LinkedIn account's organic reach. The 60 daily posts took ~30 hours and built a 1,200-follower audience. Same time, very different outcomes. The channel is the lever.

## Done-when

- 1 primary channel chosen with the 3-question audit defended in writing.
- 1 secondary channel chosen as backup, also defended.
- A weekly cadence committed: posts/week, intros/week, blog-posts/month.
- A kill-criterion written down: "if I don't see 2 paid customers from this channel by Week N, I'm switching."

If you can't fill in N and the kill-criterion, you'll spend 6 months on the wrong channel like everyone else.
