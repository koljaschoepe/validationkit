# Agency — Cold Discovery

> **Use when:** no mutual connection, but you've researched their public output.
>
> **Channel:** email if findable; LinkedIn DM otherwise.
>
> **Reply-rate expectation:** 3–8% per Track-D1 priors. Below 2% = wrong segment / wrong subject.

## Template

```
Subject: How [agency name] keeps customer agent-files in sync

Hi [first name],

I came across your [talk / case study / repo / podcast appearance] on
[exact topic] last week. The part where you described [ONE specific decision
they made] mapped to a pattern I'm researching across DACH AI agencies.

I'm doing 10 short conversations on a specific topic: how multi-customer
agencies keep AI-agent-files (CLAUDE.md / AGENTS.md / .cursor/rules) aligned
across customer repos.

25 minutes, structured, no pitch attached. The output is an open-source
playbook; your name and your agency's name appear only if you opt-in.

Tuesday Nov 18 at 11:00 or Thursday Nov 20 at 15:00 CET?

If neither works, ignore — no offence taken.

— Kolja
[link to your personal site, NOT to a product]
```

## What makes this different from cold-DM spam

Track-D1 v4 research: zero solo founders in the 8-comp-set grew sub-$5k-ACV products via LinkedIn cold-outreach. **But** discovery interviews are not sales DMs. They earn replies in the 3–8% band because:

1. **The ask is small** (25 min, no purchase).
2. **The framing is research** (handbook, not pitch).
3. **The specificity is high** (named talk, named decision).
4. **The opt-out is loud** ("ignore — no offence").

If your reply rate is below 2%, the problem isn't the channel. It's *your* specificity. Look at the message: did you actually reference something they said? Or did you reference their bio?

## Where to find good "specific decisions"

| Source | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Conference talks (Code.Talks DE, OOP München, Web Summit, Heise dev) | "We chose X over Y because…" passages |
| Podcasts (German AI / startup) | Architecture trade-offs they describe |
| GitHub repos under their org | Commits that show how they wrote agent-files |
| LinkedIn long-form posts | Lessons-learned posts |
| Annual reports / blog roundups | "Mistakes we made" posts |

## Pre-flight before sending

- [ ] Specific talk / post / repo named is real and you've actually consumed it.
- [ ] Detail you cite is *quotable* — you could read the source line aloud.
- [ ] Their agency size is in the 4–30 band.
- [ ] You've checked their public repos for *active* customer-engagement signals.
- [ ] You're sending fewer than 3 of these per day (manual respects rate).
- [ ] Row in `recruitment.md` notes which source you cited.

## When they reply with "what's this exactly?"

```
Fair — I'm researching across ~10 DACH AI agencies to map the operational
layer between customer-signature and customer-delivery. Specifically:
what's the substitute for keeping templates in sync today, what does it
cost, and where does it break first.

The output is a playbook that any agency can read (free, open). Your name and
agency name appear *only* if you opt-in at the end of the conversation.

Still up for 25 min on [time]?
```

Direct. References the research goal. Re-states the opt-in promise.

## When they reply with "we don't have that problem"

That's data. Reply:

```
Got it. Quick clarification — when you onboard a new customer who already has
some agent-file setup, how do you reconcile their existing config with your
canonical template?

If the answer is "we don't" or "case by case, manually", that's still a
substitute pattern I'd like to understand.

— Kolja
```

You're testing whether the answer was "we use a different substitute" or "the substitute is invisible to us." Either is interesting data.

## When they ignore the message

Don't follow up. The 90-day re-out reach window is real but rarely used. Move to the next row.
