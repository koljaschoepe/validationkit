# Agency — Conference / Deep-Read Follow-Up

> **Use when:** you've just seen them speak at a conference, listened to their podcast appearance, or read a long-form piece they published.
>
> **Channel:** the email they shared in the talk / show notes. LinkedIn DM as fallback.
>
> **Send within 48 hours of the event.** After that, the moment fades.
>
> **Reply-rate expectation:** 30–50%. Below 20% → too generic.

## Template

```
Subject: Your [talk / piece] on [topic] — one follow-up

Hi [first name],

I was at [conference name on date] / read your piece on [URL] yesterday.

The part where you said [exact quote, ≤ 20 words, in quotation marks] —
I've been hearing variations of that pattern from DACH AI agencies and want
to triangulate it more rigorously.

If you're open: 25 minutes, structured, no pitch. Specific topic: how
multi-customer agencies keep agent-files aligned, and what the substitute
is when there's no tool for it.

Tuesday Nov 18 at 11:00 or Thursday Nov 20 at 15:00 CET?

— Kolja
[link to your personal site, NOT to a product]
```

## Why conference follow-ups out-perform cold

The recipient's brain associates *you* with *the event they were at / piece they wrote*. That's free pre-context. The 30-50% reply rate is real because:

1. They expect follow-ups in this window.
2. The specific quote proves you were there / read it.
3. The 25-min ask is below the threshold where they'd say "send me a brief."
4. No pitch attached = lowest-risk yes.

## The "exact quote" requirement

| Done right | Done wrong |
|---|---|
| `"In our last 4 onboardings we lost 12 hours each to template-drift cleanup"` | "You talked about template drift" |
| `"We use Notion as the source of truth, which is also our biggest source of confusion"` | "Your point about Notion was great" |
| `"The 2nd customer's deviation broke our 1st customer's CI"` | "I liked your customer story" |

Specific quotes prove real engagement. Vague references read as templated outreach with the variable filled in.

## Where to find the quote

- Conference talks: most have published slides + transcripts. Search.
- Podcast appearances: most podcasters post a transcript or full show notes within a week.
- Long-form pieces: the quote is in the piece. Re-read the section that hit you.

If you can't find a real quote in 5 minutes, the moment has faded. Move on to another row.

## Pre-flight before sending

- [ ] You actually attended / listened / read. (Conference badge scans, podcast minute-timestamp, browser history.)
- [ ] The quote is verifiable (you could re-cite the source).
- [ ] Send is within 48h of the event.
- [ ] Two slots are real in your calendar.
- [ ] You're sending no more than 5 of these per conference (one per speaker you actually engaged with).

## When they reply with "great talk, thanks!"

That's a positive non-commit. Reply within 1 hour:

```
Glad it landed. The 25-min ask still open — Tuesday Nov 18 at 11:00 or
Thursday Nov 20 at 15:00 CET if you're up for it.

— Kolja
```

Brief. Re-offers the slots. No new pitch material.

## When they reply with "happy to chat — but next month?"

Yes. Take the later date. Don't push for sooner. Patience here = reply-rate compound.

```
Next month works. Pick a slot: [link to a Calendly with weekday slots only].

— Kolja
```

## What you DON'T do

- Don't pitch the product in the first message. (Conference followers expect networking, not selling.)
- Don't include a deck. (You haven't earned the open yet.)
- Don't say "I run an AI consultancy tool you might find interesting." (Wrong tense, wrong moment.)
- Don't follow up within 48h if they don't reply. Conference inboxes are flooded; if you don't make the cut, you don't make the cut.
